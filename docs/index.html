Release notes

What's new in 1.1.0

The first release-notes page for GoaSynth, and the largest update the instrument has had: the trancegate gained the classic step contours, the arp was moved onto absolute sample time, the meter went stereo, and the factory bank grew from 135 to 175 patches. Everything below is in the build you download today.

⣿

Four trancegate step shapes

The gate strip gained a SHAPE cell beside its steps: the hard SQUARE chop it always had, plus SMOOTH (half-cosine), SAW (instant in, linear fall) and TRIANGLE (sine rise, sine fall) — the contours the genre is built on. Step edges are ramp-smoothed, so the boundary between two steps stops clicking.

⇅

Arp timing, rebuilt

Note-offs are parked with absolute sample times and delivered by the block that contains them, instead of being clamped into the block that fired the note-on. A gate longer than one buffer is no longer truncated, and the STRUM fan keeps its 18 ms stagger at small buffer sizes. Every step has its own gate length (5–100 %, 75 % by default), and accents hold their gate longer as well as hitting harder.

▤

True stereo metering

The header meter now shows real per-channel peaks with peak-hold ticks — held for about 1.2 s, then falling at roughly 12 dB/s, the way a DAW meter behaves — instead of one gain-reduction strip, so you can see both sides of the image.

◍

Pink noise, and a tidier voice

NOISE gained a White / Pink choice: white for brightness, hats and transients; pink for the −3 dB/oct tilt that reads as cymbal wash, air, wind and sea. Pink is a decorrelated Paul Kellet 7-pole pair — one filter per channel — so it stays stereo.

◇

More synth, straight into the matrix

Inter-oscillator hard sync and ring mod, manual pulse width, and a three-band master EQ (low shelf, peaking mid, high shelf) with true bypass — and filter B cutoff and resonance, vowel morph, analog character, OTT depth and pump depth are all modulation destinations, so the deeper parts of the engine move like the rest of it.

⇄

Undo, A/B, randomise, lock

A 32-step snapshot stack behind UNDO / REDO, two slots behind A/B, one-button RAND with musically bounded draws, and LOCK to keep the knobs you already like through all three. Drop a WAV on an oscillator and it is sliced into wavetable frames. Editor size, zoom and skin are remembered for next time.

◈

175 patches, and a browser to find them

The bank grew from 135 to 175 patches across nine families, and the browser was rebuilt around it: favourites that persist, AND search over names and tags, sort by A-Z / Z-A / newest / bank order, click to audition, double-click to load, and a right-click menu to rename, duplicate, delete or reveal.

✦

Three new themes, and zoom

NEON (cyan and hot magenta on black), PAPER (a real light mode) and OLED (pure black, one red accent) join UV Goa, steel and warm analog, and the whole interface zooms from 100 % to 200 % from the header or with Ctrl + scroll. The active step glows as it plays.

⌗

The factory bank is exposed to your DAW's program list, the arp can send its notes out as MIDI to record or drive another instrument, and this release adds AU / AUv3 on macOS and LV2 on Linux next to VST3. Sound engine

Oscillators, wavetable drawing & voice architecture

Each voice runs two full oscillators, a sub and white noise, with a unison stack of up to seven detuned voices around both oscillators — all band-limited so a scream stays a scream, not an alias.

Oscillator A / B

  • Waves: Saw, Square, PWM, Triangle, Sine — or a user-drawn wavetable
  • Octave ±2 and fine detune ±50 cents
  • Level and independent pan per oscillator
  • Start phase 0–360° plus phase randomisation for wide, non-identical stacks
  • FM: oscillator B phase-modulates oscillator A — metallic, bell and vocal formants from a single knob

Wavetable morphing & drawing

  • 8 frames per oscillator. Pick a frame in the strip under the display and sketch it: left-drag draws, right-drag erases, double-click resets.
  • WT POS sweeps the table with linear frame interpolation; the other frames show as ghost outlines and the display previews the live morphed output.
  • Edit tools on each oscillator: SMOOTH (blur), FLIP (polarity), NORM (re-level) and the P25 / P50 / FORMANT / SPIKES shape generators.
  • Transforms remove DC and normalise, so even scratchy doodles play at full level.
  • Both LFOs can target WT POS A and WT POS B for evolving spectral movement.

Filtering

Screaming 303 resonance, two filters deep

Filter A

  • Types: LP 12 dB, LP 24 dB, HP 12 dB, BP 12 dB, Notch — TPT state-variable
  • Cutoff 20 Hz – 20 kHz with resonance, drive and key tracking
  • Filter envelope amount ±5 octaves, drawn on a draggable graph

Filter B + routing

An independent second filter — its own type, cutoff and resonance — with a ROUTE selector that changes the character completely:

SERIALB follows A. The classic cascade — a 24 dB lowpass into a second stage for a steep, squelching 303 sweep.
PARALLELA and B summed 50/50. A lowpass plus a highpass becomes a band limiter; two bandpasses gives a formant notch.
SPLITA filters the left channel, B the right — wide stereo filter movement you can hear panning across the image.

Filter character

  • F-DRIVE saturates before the main filter, so a screaming resonance stays musical instead of clicking and clipping its way through the envelope
  • F-FB feeds a one-sample of the filter output back into its own input. Low settings thicken; high settings are the screech-and-scream zone psy leads live in, and it reaches self-oscillation at the top. A hard safety clamp means it can never run away on you

Vowel / formant filter

A three-formant bank sits after the main filters and morphs through AH → EH → EE → OH → OO on one knob — talking acid, vocal pads and phased hoovers with no third-party plugin in the chain.

  • VOWEL switches the bank in, MORPH walks the sequence, V-RES sharpens the formants and V-MIX blends dry against filtered
  • Both LFOs can target VOWEL, so the morph can move on its own — a slow LFO gives you vowel drift, a fast one gives you a talkbox

Chorus & phaser

Rate / depth / mix each. The chorus widens supersaws; the phaser gives a slow moving sweep behind acid lines.

Tempo-synced ping-pong delay

Off, 1/16, 1/8 dotted, 1/8, 1/4 or free milliseconds. Feedback runs through a 5 kHz lowpass so repeats darken the way analog echoes do instead of turning brittle.

Reverb

Size and damping into a straightforward mix control. Long, dark tails for morning pads; barely there for tight bass. SHIM grows an octave-up pitch-shifted copy of the tail — the shimmer that hangs over a breakdown — so a held pad keeps rising after the note has stopped.

Movement & noise

Three more panels sit on the FX row. MOVEMENT gathers the analog drift amount, the unison detune and stereo width, and the FX duck in one place — the slow per-voice wander that stops a supersaw sounding like a photocopy. MACRO holds two performance knobs meant as modulation-matrix sources: MIDI CC 14 / CC 15 drive them out of the box, so a single controller sweep can move any routed destination. NOISE is the voice's white-noise level on its own knob, for hats, wind and riser beds without borrowing an oscillator. The SUB panel's CHAR cell reshapes the unison field itself (CLASSIC spread, PHASED centre bunching, HYPER widened edges) and CHORD makes single keys sound scale-snapped chords.

OTT multiband compressor

The signal is split at 220 Hz and 2 kHz with fourth-order crossovers, and each band gets the classic fast upward + downward squeeze that makes psy kickbasses and saws feel glued and loud. DEPTH blends from fully transparent to the full psy squeeze, LOW / MID / HIGH trim each band's response and OUT is an output pad.

Depth 0 is a true DSP bypass — no filter colouration when you don't want it. Every effect in the chain is a real parameter, so your DAW can automate the whole signal path.

Master EQ — low shelf, peaking mid, high shelf

A three-band EQ sits after MASTER and before the limiter, so it shapes the signal the limiter then catches rather than being re-shaped after the fact: a low shelf at 200 Hz, a peaking mid with its own frequency control (150 Hz – 6 kHz) and a high shelf at 4 kHz, each ±15 dB. It is the last thing you reach for on a finished patch — take off the mud, push the presence, keep the limiter honest.

Every band defaults to 0 dB, and an all-zero EQ is a true bypass: no coefficients are computed and no filtering happens, so a patch that ignores it cannot be coloured by it.

Sidechain pump — the kick-follow feel, with nothing to route

Set PUMP to 1/4, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16 or 1/16T and the output ducks on that division, locked to the host transport: deepest right on the beat, then an exponential recovery until the next one. DEPTH sets how far it dips, so a nudge gives you movement and a full turn gives you the classic Goa pump. It sits on the voice output ahead of the FX, which is why the reverb tails breathe with it instead of fighting it — and why you never have to build a sidechain bus, add a mixer channel or hope your host has a kick track to trigger from. Off — sync at OFF or depth at zero — is a true bypass.

Scale, root and lock

Two cells beside the strip pick a SCALE and a ROOT, and every programmed step is snapped into that scale (ties resolve downward, which is the darker, more musical choice). Turn on LOCK and the same quantiser applies to notes you play live — hold a chord and it folds into Phrygian instead of the chromatic mush your keyboard hands you.

FILL — a roll in the key you're in

One click on the FILL cell writes three to six in-scale steps into the empty positions, leaving every step you programmed alone, so a sparse pattern becomes a psy roll without you plotting sixteen cells by hand. It only ever uses notes of the current SCALE and ROOT — including the pentatonics, where that constraint is what makes a roll sound deliberate — and it deliberately skips some rests instead of filling every gap, because a roll with air in it is the sound, not a machine-gun.

The playhead cell lights up while the transport runs, so you can see exactly where you are.

Tuning

In the scale you meant, in the tuning you want

Two things most synths leave to luck: whether a rolling arp stays in key, and what "in tune" means at all. GoaSynth answers both from the controls at the bottom-right of the window — nine scale tables, a root selector, and full Scala microtuning.

Scale quantizer

Pick a table with the SCALE cell and a pitch class with ROOT. Every semitone the arp strip plays is snapped into that scale, and an out-of-scale step lands on the nearest in-scale note — ties resolve downward, which is usually the answer the ear wanted anyway. A row of twelve LEDs beside the cell shows both the mode and what your pattern does with it: in-scale degrees glow faintly, the pitch classes your enabled arp steps actually hit light up, and the root is the brightest — so you can read the shape of the scale and the shape of the line at a glance.

  • chromatic (off)
  • minor
  • Phrygian
  • harmonic minor
  • Hungarian minor
  • double harmonic
  • Dorian
  • major
  • minor pentatonic

LOCK lifts the quantiser off the arp and puts it on your playing: hold a chord and every note folds into the selected scale. With LOCK on and Hungarian minor chosen, a wrong key never lands — it simply becomes the note of the scale that sits closest to it.

Microtuning — Scala .scl

FINE¢ shifts the whole instrument by up to ±100 cents, so −32 puts you on a 432 Hz reference and everything else in the session follows. The SCL button goes further and loads a Scala .scl file: the classic text format, 2 to 64 degrees per octave, octave-wrapped and anchored on A4.

  • Accepts what real files contain — cent values (204.0), ratios (6/5) and decimal ratios (1.25), with comments and blank lines ignored
  • Any equal or unequal temperament: just intonation, 19-tone, 31-tone, gamelan tables, hand-built per-octave scales
  • The button takes the name of the loaded tuning and shows its degree count on hover; clicking again returns to 12-TET
  • Your file is copied to %APPDATA%/GoaSynth/tuning.scl and reloaded next session, so the tuning survives even if you move or delete the original
  • Tunings swap on a lock-free note table — the UI publishes, the audio thread keeps reading — so changing scale between tracks never gaps or clicks

Octave wrapping means a seven-degree file still plays every key on the keyboard: notes above its range continue the pattern instead of falling back to 12-TET.

Why this genre needs it: Goa and psy trance are built on hypnotic repetition, so one wrong step is audible by the fourth bar. Scale locks keep a wild arp in key without flattening it, and microtuning is how a lead sits against a tabla, a flute or a 432 Hz drone instead of beating against it.

OPENAIAny OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint — GPT-5.6 Luna by default, with GPT-5 Mini and the legacy 4o-mini selectable.
CUSTOMA base URL you own: Ollama or LM Studio on http://localhost:11434/v1, OpenRouter, or anything else speaking the same API. A local server needs no key at all.

Your key is stored per user in %APPDATA%/GoaSynth/ai.txt on your own disk, and it — together with your prompt — goes only to the provider you selected, never to us. What leaves your machine is the brief plus a compact table of parameter names; your presets, projects and audio never do. Replies are parsed defensively before anything reaches the synth: unknown parameters are dropped, numbers clamped and bogus choice names rejected.

It repairs its own bad reply

A model occasionally answers with a parameter that doesn't exist or a value outside its range. Instead of handing you a half-broken patch, the plugin shows the model its own rejected reply and exactly what was wrong with it, and asks once more — so a formality slip costs a second rather than a generation. If the second reply fails validation too, nothing is applied and you are told why.

The whole engine is in its vocabulary

Generated patches are not limited to the classics: the designer can reach the vowel morph, the scale quantiser, the fine tuning, the pump and the quality switch, so "talking acid in Phrygian" is a brief it can actually answer rather than a stretch.

And it learns the character you keep

Every cloud patch you keep is archived on disk — the brief, the model's title and the values it produced — in %APPDATA%/GoaSynth/Learned. Later LOCAL generations are blended with the best-matching entries, so the offline designer gradually picks up the stamp of the models you actually liked, with no key and no network. Nothing in that archive is ever transmitted, and deleting the folder resets the memory.

Honest about the name: the default engine is a rule-based generator, not a language model — no weights, no server, no key — so it costs nothing and works on a machine that has never been online. Cloud generation is a switch only you can flip, with your own key, billed by your own provider, and every cloud patch you keep quietly improves the offline one.

Preset packs

EXPORT PACK and IMPORT PACK move whole banks in and out of the plugin. A pack is a .goapack file — a plain ZIP containing .goapreset files — so it's trivial to email, torrent or archive.

  • Export writes every patch in your merged bank (own presets, plus shared-bank patches and their tags) into a single pack, behind a native overwrite prompt.
  • Import merges non-destructively: existing presets are skipped, so a pack can never silently clobber what you have. New ones appear in the browser immediately.
  • Junk entries (READMEs, __MACOSX folders) are ignored, and zip paths are flattened on import so nested or hostile entries can't write outside the presets folder.
  • Packs round-trip: export → send → import → play.

Contributing

UI conventions

The editor keeps its hover info and modulation indicators uniform on purpose. If you touch Source/PluginEditor, these are the rules new controls follow.

Tooltips

Every interactive control has hover text, displayed by the single TooltipWindow the editor owns — without it every setTooltip() is a silent no-op. Knobs and toggles build theirs automatically: parameter name + live value in engine units (Cutoff (Hz) — 1500 Hz, Attack — 0.01 s, RESO — 25 %; toggles show ON/OFF). The unit map is liveValueText() in Source/PluginEditor.cpp; give any new parameter a branch there, or the OverlayTest unit sweep fails and names the id. Text is rebuilt every paint, so values track drags, host automation and preset loads while you hover — never cache tooltip strings. Hand-written tooltips follow the house style: what it is, an em dash, then the gestures.

Mod dots & pick-to-assign

A knob targeted by an active mod-matrix slot wears small dots in its top-right corner — one per source slot (max 3): teal for LFO 1, magenta for LFO 2, accent for envelopes, dim for velocity/modwheel. Each dot pulses with the live source value × amount, and depth past ±0.6 adds a ring. Hovering the knob lists its routings (MOD 2: LFO 1 → CUTOFF (+40)). Dots refresh on matrix edits, overlay open and preset loads — any new code path that changes slots must call goaui::repaintCtlPaints().

The matrix itself routes by pointing: click a DST cell then a knob, or a SRC cell then an LFO/ENV graph; ESC or a re-click cancels, and the just-routed cell flashes inside the card with a brief arrow pointing at the clicked control to confirm. New routable controls must expose their parameter ids (Ctl::paramId, LfoGraph::rateParamId, EnvGraph::attackParamId()).
